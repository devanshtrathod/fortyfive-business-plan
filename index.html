05 · Coursework

Help with the IA, EE and TOK, inside the IB's rules

We don't sell coursework and nobody here writes it. Students book a coach by the hour to learn the skills the criteria reward, and the Marker, an AI, tells them where a draft stands, criterion by criterion, without writing a word of it.

The line, in one sentence. A coach can teach anything the student needs to know and ask any question that makes them think, but never writes, edits, rewrites, translates or marks up the work itself. Every session leaves a log the student can show their supervisor.
A coach does
  • Explain each criterion and what separates the levels, in their own words.
  • Help the student find and narrow their own topic and research question, by asking questions.
  • Teach methods: research, judging sources, collecting data, the statistics or analysis the subject expects, referencing.
  • Teach the subject knowledge the piece needs, like how a chi-squared test works or what makes a TOK example strong.
  • Plan: milestones, a word budget for each section, how to act on the supervisor's feedback.
  • Use public or made-up examples to show what strong work looks like, as the IB itself suggests.
  • Get the student ready to talk about their own work, which is how teachers check it's theirs.
A coach never
×
Writes, rewrites, edits, proofreads, translates or "polishes" any part of the work.
×
Takes the file, marks up the draft or predicts its grade. No files change hands; a student can screen-share a plan to talk it through.
×
Supplies a research question, thesis, data, analysis or sources the student hasn't found.
×
Runs the experiment, the survey or the calculations.
×
Shares their own IA, EE or TOK, or anyone else's.
×
Coaches a student whose school bans outside help with coursework.

Why the line sits here: the IB's academic integrity policy says that when a student gets help from tutors "beyond what is recommended in the subject guidelines", the work is "not entirely produced by the student", and it asks every school to set its own rules on tutors. Its AI appendix wants students to be able to "speak about their work with their teachers". Coaching that leaves a student able to explain every choice is the safe kind.

The FortyFive Marker

an AI that marks against the criteria and writes nothing for you
Practice marking · from April 2027

Marks answers to our own practice questions and predicted papers, against our own markschemes. Included in every course. No coursework involved, so no integrity risk, and it does what people couldn't afford to: mark every practice answer.

Coursework check · subject by subject

A student uploads an IA, EE or TOK draft and gets a criterion report. ₹499 a piece for two checks: one on a draft and one before submission, at least a week apart. Free for anyone who asks. A subject opens only once it passes the accuracy bar below.

Teacher mode · with school plans

A teacher runs a class set of drafts through the Marker for a first pass, then writes their own feedback faster and marks more consistently across the department. The teacher always has the final word.

What a report shows

  • A band for each criterion and how sure it is: "3 to 4 out of 6, fairly sure".
  • The evidence behind each judgement: the page and paragraph it read.
  • Two or three things that work, and two or three questions to ask yourself.
  • A checklist: research question stated, word count, sources cited, data processing shown, evaluation, a conclusion that answers the question.
  • A ready line to credit the AI feedback, and a link to share the report with the supervisor.

What it never shows

×
Sentences to paste, rewritten paragraphs or a "fixed" version.
×
A research question, a thesis or an argument.
×
A chat box. It writes a report and stops.
×
One confident number when its three passes disagree. Then it shows a range.
×
A promise of a grade.

Accuracy, published

After results day, graduates can donate their final IA, EE or TOK with the mark the IB gave it (their consent, and a parent's if they're under 18). That becomes the test set.

  • A subject's coursework check opens only when, on at least 30 real pieces for each component, the Marker's total lands within 2 marks of the IB's mark for 7 pieces in 10. Set the bar with an IB teacher.
  • Every subject page shows the error for each criterion, updated after every session, misses included.
  • RevisionDojo's own blog says there's no published dataset comparing its grader with final IB marks. Publishing ours is the edge.
Why only two checks

IB subject guides let a teacher comment on one draft. If a student can run their work through an AI twenty times and change whatever it flags, the result starts to be the AI's work, and the IB doesn't count work produced even partly by AI as the student's own (Academic integrity policy, Appendix 6). Two checks a week apart help without taking over.

The donated pieces later become an exemplar library: read on FortyFive only, watermarked, with notes on why each criterion scored what it did. The IB says students benefit from seeing a good example, as long as they don't copy it.

What we learned from RevisionDojo

Their three coursework pages are mirrored in site-templates (port 8830), with a teardown in _notes/08-revisiondojo.md.

  • Grader: an 8-step upload (type, subject, file, rubric and level, draft or finished, language and citation style, short or full report, privacy), then a score for each criterion, notes pinned on the PDF and next steps. It marks three times and averages, deletes files after 48 hours and asks for an own-work certificate.
  • Coursework Studio: a workspace behind the login: set up the piece, research and plan, drafts, feedback, revise and reflect, deadlines. Free users get a capped number of projects.
  • Exemplars: "thousands" of past pieces with scores they say IB examiners verified, filtered by type, subject and language, nearly all behind Pro.

Take: the stepped upload, draft versus final, marks for each criterion, several passes, 48-hour deletion, the own-work certificate. Skip: unpublished accuracy, unlimited rounds, a paywall on everything, the mascot.

Rules for this part

07 · Safety: the most important page

Teaching teenagers online: get this right before anything else

Most students on FortyFive will be 15 to 17, and most tutors will be adults or recent graduates their parents have never met. Safety comes before features, growth and fees.

The safeguarding lead must be an adult. You're 17, so it should be one of your parents (or another adult on the team): named on the website, easy to reach, and trained. They decide on reports; you build the system that makes reporting easy.

Who gets to tutor (18 and over)

  • ID check through a verification service (DigiLocker or similar). Never store Aadhaar numbers.
  • Background check: a criminal records search, about ₹1,000. FortyFive pays for the founding tutors.
  • The official IB results document, or teaching credentials.
  • A reference from a teacher.
  • A 15-minute video interview and demo lesson with you or a subject lead.
  • 30 minutes of safeguarding training and a signed code of conduct.
Graduate tutors under 18

  • A verified 7 in every subject they teach, from the official results document. Adults can teach with a 6; under-18s can't.
  • A parent or guardian co-signs the tutor agreement, because a minor's contract is void in India, and payouts go to the parent's bank account.
  • Their own parent sees their bookings and messages too, so an adult is watching both sides of every lesson. They need a parent's consent for their own data as well (DPDP).
  • A reference from their DP Coordinator or head of school instead of a criminal-records check, plus the same interview, demo lesson and safeguarding training.
  • DP students only: no adult learners and no lessons booked by adults for themselves. Online on FortyFive only, 7 am to 9 pm.
  • Labelled honestly: "Graduate tutor, 17" on the profile, so families choose knowingly.
  • At 18 they move to the standard track: full ID and background check, payouts to their own account.

The lawyer checks this track before launch. Ask about insurance cover for tutors under 18.

How lessons with under-18s work

  • Lessons only in FortyFive's video room; messages only in FortyFive's chat.
  • Phone numbers, emails and social handles are hidden in chat automatically.
  • The parent's account sees every booking and message, and the parent can join any lesson.
  • Lessons only between 7 am and 9 pm.
  • Parents choose at sign-up whether lessons are recorded (coursework coaching is always recorded, until results). Recordings are kept for 30 days, opened only if someone reports a problem, then deleted.
  • No gifts, no social media follows, no meeting in person through FortyFive.

When something goes wrong

  1. A report button in every lesson and every chat, for students, parents and tutors.
  2. The safeguarding lead reads every report within 24 hours, and anything about a child's safety the same day.
  3. If you learn a child may have been abused: suspend the tutor, tell the parents and report to the police or the Special Juvenile Police Unit straight away. Childline is 1098.
  4. Write down what happened and what was done, then fix the rule that failed.

Under POCSO, anyone who knows of a sexual offence against a child must report it (section 19). Not reporting is itself an offence (section 21), with a heavier penalty for a person in charge of an organisation. Courts have said an internal inquiry can't come first.

Academic integrity

  • Tutors teach content, skills and how the criteria work.
  • Coursework coaching follows the line in section 05: coaches teach and ask, and never write, edit, rewrite, translate or mark up anyone's IA, EE or TOK. No files change hands.
  • Every coaching session is recorded and logged, and the log goes to the student and parent to show the supervisor.
  • Graduate tutors under 18 don't coach coursework.
  • Crossing the line ends a tutor's account. It protects students too: unauthorised help can cost them their diploma.

Children's data (DPDP Rules)

The children's parts apply from about May 2027: verifiable consent from a parent for under-18 users, and no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads aimed at children.

  • The parent creates the account and gives consent.
  • Progress is a checklist the student ticks, not tracking.
  • No ads aimed at students: talk to parents and make good content.
  • Collect the minimum, delete on request, and name a contact for complaints.

17 · Rules and risks

Six rules that are never broken

×
Never put IB past papers, markschemes or pages copied from them on FortyFive, paywalled or not. Every 2026 IB markscheme says: "Use by third parties, including but not limited to publishers, private teachers, tutoring or study services… whether fee-covered or not, is prohibited and is a criminal offense."
×
Never use SaveMyExams, InThinking, LitCharts or teachers' material in a course. Charging for access instead of files doesn't change who owns it.
×
Nobody on FortyFive writes, edits, rewrites or marks up anyone's IA, EE or TOK. Coaches teach the skills by the hour, every session is logged for the supervisor, and the Marker gives criterion feedback, never text to paste. One coursework scandal ends the project and can cost a student their diploma.
×
No contact between tutors and under-18s outside FortyFive. No phone numbers, no WhatsApp, no social media, no meetings.
×
No claim that can't be proved. Scores, results, reviews and numbers are real or they don't go up.
×
No "IB" in the brand or logo. Say "for IB Diploma students" and add: "Not affiliated with or endorsed by the International Baccalaureate Organization."
